#ethics-security
Evelyn and Leo, the founders of NextGen Robotics, have just completed their latest project: MechaMates, an advanced robot companion that can assist with household tasks, answer questions about various topics, and even play interactive games. They're thrilled to launch MechaMates to consumers worldwide, but some unexpected issues have arisen.

First, a week after launching, Evelyn and Leo realised they overlooked the terms in a contract they signed with a supplier who provided some of the robotic hardware. Now they're concerned they might have breached the contract terms without realising it.

Additionally, one of their early customers, Alex Tran, discovered that MechaMates can be manipulated into performing unauthorised tasks if prompted in certain ways. MechaMates doesn't fully recognise trick questions and will sometimes execute commands it shouldn't exposing a potential security risk.

To complicate matters, NextGen Robotics has received a cease-and-desist letter from an inventor claiming that MechaMates infringes on their patent for a game-playing robot. Evelyn and Leo aren't sure if the claim is legitimate, but they're anxious to avoid a legal dispute. 

Lastly, Evelyn has been telling Leo that they need better cybersecurity in their office, especially after hearing about recent data breaches at other tech companies. However, Leo believes that because they store little sensitive data, cybersecurity isn't a major concern.

##### Question A (5 Marks)
> Evelyn and Leo are worried they may have breached a contract with their hardware supplier. Explain the importance of Contracts and NDAs in engineering projects and what steps they should take if they suspect a contract violation.
- **Why are contracts important?**
	Contracts define the legal responsibilities between one or more parties. In the context of an engineering project, a contract may commonly used to define the responsibilities and liabilities of a supplier of components or manufacturer of the product. This ensures that the products/components are always up to a certain standard, and are not otherwise manufactured out of specification or for a client outside of the commissioning client.
- **Why are NDAs important?**
	NDAs are an agreement to keep certain confidential information secret, between two parties. In the example above, an NDA may be used to ensure any in-progress designs remain secret. For example, a prototype design may be sent to NextGen Robotics' manufacturing partner to allow for the evaluation of the manufactured parts before mass-manufacture. This prevents designs that may have yet to make it to market from falling into the hands of competitors.
- **What to do if contract violation?**
	If Evelyn or Leo suspect they have violated a contract signed with a parts supplier, they should first ensure that they have reasonably interpreted the words of the contract, perhaps by running it past a lawyer specialising in contract law. If a breach of the contract is then confirmed, the other parties to the contract should be notified of a breach of contract on the part of NextGen Robotics. From here some sort of remediation will likely take place. Depending on the word of the contract and the severity of the breach, this may be simple and just requires meetings with the part supplier. However, if more severe, then lawyers are likely to get involved, whether that be through some sort of arbitration (which may be forced by the contract) or by taking the contract breach to court. The latter option is more likely to be filed by the part supplier, instead of NextGen Robotics. The resultant actions would then be decided by a court.
##### Question B (5 Marks)
> MechaMates can be tricked into performing unauthorised tasks due to social engineering vulnerabilities. Explain what social engineering is and suggest ways that Evelyn and Leo can update MechaMates to better defined against these type of attacks. (5 Marks)
- Social engineering is a type of attack by which a person may gain unauthorised information, or cause actions to take place that they should not be able to by abusing the social nature of humans. In this case, it is likely that MechaMates are not abusing humans as the vulnerability, but some form of artificial intelligence. As artificial intelligence, depending on the type used, which appears to be some sort of LLM here, is unable to perform accurate reasoning as to what information or actions it should or should not do when prompted to by a given user, it is not guaranteed that a protective pre-prompt would prevent it from exposing any information it has access to to a malicious user. 
- Thus, the most effective way to prevent access to this kind of information or to sensitive actions is to put it behind some non-AI checker. For instance, the AI may request some information on information about the system it is running on. While it is not possible at present to prevent the AI from 'hallucinating' an answer, it is also possible to allow for the AI to attempt to access the data through an API, which should have some sort of traditional credential checker.  
- Another important step to take is to ensure that the AI does not have sensitive data within its training dataset. This is because AIs are liable to repeat, verbatim or otherwise with few modifications any input in its training dataset. Furthermore, if the AI continues to train based on user input, it is important to disable this for any testing of the AI, as this may require inputting data that may be sensitive.
