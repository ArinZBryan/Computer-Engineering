### Contracts
A *contract* is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties that outlies each parties' obligations, rights and requirements. Contracts may be written, verbal or implied, though a written contract is much easier to enforce and litigate.
When entering into a contract, each party must voluntarily consent to the terms of the contract, and that they must each be legally capable of entering into such an agreement. If a contract is breached (one party fails to perform as stated in the contract), then the parties can attempt to reconcile. Generally, that may involve going to court.
##### Some Terms
**Unilateral vs Bilateral**
- In *unilateral* contracts, one party only is promising something to the other.
- In *bilateral contracts*, each party is promising something to each other.
**Express vs Implied**
- In *express* contracts, all the terms are stipulated ahead of time
- In *implied* contracts, the terms of the contracts are left unsaid, and need to be implied from actions, conduct or reasonable belief.
**Executed vs Executory**
A contract that is *executed*, all parties have fulfilled their obligations. Otherwise, the contract is *executory*.
### NDAs
A *Non-Disclosure Agreement* is a legal confidential relationship between two parties. This allows for the protection of secrets and other sensitive information from being leaked to third-parties.
In an NDA, there are three main parts:
- Parties involved
	- Disclosing Party
	- Receiving Party
	Lots of NDAs will have both parties as disclosing and receiving parties.
- Definition of confidential information
	Defines what information is considered confidential. May also define specific exclusions from confidentiality.
- Obligations of security
	Outlines the minimum measures each party must take to ensure that the confidential information remains confidential.
- Contract Duration
	How long you have to keep quiet for.
- Consequences for breaches
	What happens if one party breaches the contract and discloses information they shouldn't.
##### Use Cases
- Business Negotiations
	Protect information during buyouts, mergers or other partnerships
- Employment
	Prevent information shared with employees from leaking
- Joint Ventures
- Consulting
### Liability
When defining a contract where one party is providing a service or product to another, the liabilities of the providing party are usually defined. More specifically, liability refers to the responsibility that the providing party has in the event that their product or service in some way causes harm or damages.
Such liabilities may or may not cover damages caused by defects, malfunctions or security vulnerabilities. Thus, in general, it's separated into three categories:
- Contractual Liability
	Liabilities arising due to breaches in contract
- Tort Liability
	Liabilities arsing due to negligence, misrepresentation or misuse of a product
- Product Liability
	Liabilities arising due to the product. This is most commonly used when a service is being provided, but it is to be treated as a product.
