#ethics-security/data 
### Digital Security
- **Cyber Security** is the practice of protecting computers, network and data from unauthorised access
- **Physical Security** is the methods used to protect the computers physically from unauthorised access.
- **Data Security** is the methods used to protect data from unauthorised access, theft or corruption. It is considered a subset of cyber security. 
- **Secure Computing** is a term used to encapsulate cyber security, physical security and data security.
##### Security as a foundation
This is a practice that describes the implementation of security measures from the outset and continuing on through the development and use of the system being secured. Building for security by default allows for it to be easier to be secure, and reduces the number of points of ingress for issues and egress for data.
##### Security Threat Landscape
- **Cybercrime** - A general term for crimes committed that are facilitated partially or completely by the internet.
- **Cyber Espionage** - Secretly gathering information, often for competitive advantage, or other harmful intent.
- **Nation State / Terrorist Attacks** - Attacks supported by, funded by or operated by nation states or terrorist groups.
##### CIA Triad
**Confidentiality** - Data should be kept secret, or otherwise to its intended recipients
**Integrity** - Data should be accurate and have providence.
**Availability** - Data should be available for access by the authorised person(s)
### Physical Security
Physical security primarily attempts to protect against the following:
- **Damage**
	- Intentional Damage
		- Assault + Battery
		- Water
	- Unintentional Damage
		- Water
		- Fire
		- Drops
- **Theft**
	- Stealing all or part of the system
- **Unauthorised Access**

##### Layers of physical security
- Outer (Perimeter)
- Inner (Facility)
- Innermost (Facility)
- Device
###### Perimeter Security
This often contains clear boundaries and visible deterrents, for example walls, fences, wire, controlled entry points (gates/barriers) and warning signs. This layer also often contains surveillance, be that by CCTV, human guards, or even just someone watching out a window. 
###### Inner Security
Often contains methods such as keyed/keycard doors, more intense surveillance, visitor management, usually in the form of a reception and physical barriers, such as turnstiles.
###### Facility Security
Access controls here are often very strict, and these areas are generally continuously monitored 24/7. If security is breached here, intrusion detection systems may set off silent alarms. Generally, MFA is needed to get around and into these areas.
###### Device Security
Generally, once an attacker is physically at the device, the game is up, but there are still some more mechanisms that can be used. Anti-theft measures, uninterruptable power supplies, tamper-proof seals and physical locks are commonly employed here.

### Social Engineering
##### In-Person
**Impersonation** - Pretending to be someone you aren't or pretending to have something (such as access rights) you don't.
**Tailgating** - Following someone through a door or other access control
**Pretexting** - Using an assumption (which may not be true) to develop trust in an area. For example, pretending you are an elevator technician to get into otherwise secure areas under the pretext of 'fixing things'.
**Shoulder Surfing** - Watching what a person is doing over their shoulders. This can also be done digitally, using for example keyloggers, or poorly placed security cameras.
**Elicitation** - Gaining information through casual conversation. Often well used against receptionists.
##### Online
**Phishing** - Send out messages attempting to trick individuals in a group to do something.
**Spear Phishing** - Phishing used on a specific person or person(s).
**Baiting** - Offering some kind of incentive to do something they shouldn't.
**Scareware** - Scaring victims into action, usually by using urgency or some other consequences.
**Fake Websites / Apps** - Tricking victims into using your service, believing that they are using the original they're really trying to use.
