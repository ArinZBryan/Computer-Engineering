##### Why Bother Securing Data?
In the event of a data breach, there can be wide-ranging consequences for the company breached. For example, the primary issue from a commercial standpoint is the lack of trust. When trust is lost, it is incredibly difficult to regain it and when a breach occurs, there are several sources and reminders to the public that they should not continue to trust a given company. For instance, the story may remain in the press of weeks, and even when the initial wave dies down, further investigations from the relevant watchdogs and regulatory bodies often result in more press coverage. Further, such investigations often lead to law-suits, fines or other penalties. These consequences can be more than just large fines, this can also involve longer-term penalties, such as requirements to provide services for free, or more red-tape and paperwork to do anything even vaguely security related. This then often then has secondary impacts, such as difficulty hiring, and significant loss of talent.
### Software
- Malware
	- Viruses - Catch-all for self-replicating software
	- Ransomware - Software that encrypts data, and ransoms it, usually for money
	- Scareware - Software that attempts to scare the user into performing actions that are ill-advised
	- Spyware - Software that attempts to spy on the user's actions
	- Trojans - Software that pretends to be something innocuous.
	- Adware - Software that serves the victim advertisements, to earn the creator ad-revenue.
- Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS)
	Spamming an endpoint with requests to prevent legitimate attempts to access a service or resource. DoS refers to a single computer performing this attack, whereas DDoS refers to a group of computers, often spread throughout the world performing the attack.
- Phishing
- Man In The Middle
	By compromising a middleman in some data transfer, it is possible, usually to monitor and spy on communications, when the channel is assumed to be secure.
- SQL Injection
	By supplying malicious database access code (usually SQL) to a user-accessible input, it may be possible to mangle an expected database request to perform actions it should not.
- Zero-Day Exploits
	This refers to an exploit that has been in a product since day-zero, that is, since the product was first released to the product.
- Social Engineering
	Misusing the trust or assumptions of humans to achieve some goal. [See More](Security%20Principles.md#Social%20Engineering).
- Advanced Persistent Threats
	This is a type of threat that is very rare, and often only targets massive companies, or those affiliated with or part of the government. Generally, these types of attacks are perpetrated by nation states or terrorist groups.
- Insider Threat
	One of the most common threats to software security is insiders, as they are implicitly trusted, they are able to perform actions without scrutiny or relevant checks and balances. Though it is not uncommon that the threat comes from intentionally malicious insiders, but it is also possible that well-meaning individuals become compromised in some way, or simply by carelessness.

### Hardware
- Chip Designs
	Many such vulnerabilities are often not intentional, but can lead to data breaches. For example, [spectre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectre_(security_vulnerability)) and [meltdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meltdown_(security_vulnerability)) vulnerabilities on Intel Core Series Chips circa 2018
- Timing Attacks
- Specific Hardware Targets
	In the event of attackers being nation-states or terror groups, specific hardware may be targeted to get a specific result. An example of such an attack is [Stuxnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuxnet).
- Supply Chain
	Some trusted supplier is compromised, allowing for otherwise secure devices to be compromised.
