#hardware/electronics/circuits 
### Ohm's Law
The potential difference across a conductor is equal to the resistance times the current floweing across it.
$$V=IR$$
Conductance is sometimes useful in calculations, it is given as $G$, and is simply the inverse of resistance, it has units of 'Siemens', or otherwise $\text{Ohm}^{-1}$.
$$G=\frac{1}{R}$$
### Kirchhoff's Voltage Law

> [!quote] The sum of all potential differences in a loop is zero

This is based on the conservation of energy - charges gain energy from power sources and loose energy doing work in components. Once they've gone around the circuit, they should end with the same energy they started with.
### Kirchhoff's Current Law

> [!quote] The sum of all currents leaving or entering a node is zero

This is based on the conservation of charge - charges are never created or destroyed, only move around. Thus, the rate of charges entering a node must be equal to the number leaving it.
